## Summary main findings
Data for 2014 to 2025 shows that favourable environments for innovation and growth are heavily concentrated in
South East England, though major city regions across the wider UK also perform strongly.

The top-scoring travel-to-work areas (TTWAs) center on southern hubs like Oxford, London, Reading, and Cambridge.
High-performing areas outside the South East include:

* Aberdeen 
* Brighton 
* Bristol 
* Edinburgh 
* Leamington Spa 
* Leeds 
* Milton Keynes 
* Southampton 
* York

Most large cities – including Birmingham, Glasgow, Manchester, and Sheffield – are in the top 30% nationwide.

Areas with the least favourable environments (bottom 5%) for innovation and growth include geographically remote
coastal towns with small populations, such as:

* Boston 
* Bude 
* Clacton 
* Great Yarmouth
* Skegness
* Whitby

Doncaster is the only major city to score particularly poorly.

## About the data

### Data source(s)
Labour Force Survey (LFS), Office for National Statistics

### Time period
* 2014 to 2025 combined

### Geographic area
UK

### What the data measures
This page combines data from the following 3 indicators into a single 'composite' score:

* Estimated proportion of higher degrees among economically active people aged 25 to 64 
* Estimated proportion of ‘new economy’ occupations among economically active people aged 25 to 64 
* Gross value added per employed worker in the travel to work area (TTWA), in million pounds

### Things you need to know
Areas are based on where respondents lived at the time they were surveyed, not where they grew up.

The unit used for this index is the 'Z-score'.
This is a type of statistical measurement which can be used to tell us how likely it is that a region is different to the UK average. 

The values shown are not percentages, and the difference between regions cannot be compared in percentage terms. 

The Z-scores are used to put regions into one of 7 groups, according to a set of thresholds for each group.
See our [technical annex]()
for more information on how each area was scored.

Gross value added per capita is calculated by each employed worker within the TTWA.
This is rather than per head of the resident population, as in the local authorities composite index.
By measuring productivity based on the number of people working in an area rather than the total number of residents,
we avoid skewed results in places where people commute in from elsewhere.

### Type of data
Survey data

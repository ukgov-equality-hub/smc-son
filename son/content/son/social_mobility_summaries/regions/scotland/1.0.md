All areas are scored according to how favourable their environments are for the following 4 measures of social mobility: Promising Prospects, Conditions of Childhood, Labour Market Opportunities for Young People and Innovation and Growth. Each measure of social mobility combines data from multiple indicators into a single &lsquo;composite&rsquo; score. The scoring bands range from &lsquo;Bottom 5%&rsquo; to &lsquo;Top 5%&rsquo;, with a large majority of areas in the &lsquo;30%-70%&rsquo; band because most local authorities are very near the average.

## Promising Prospects

This index measures how well young people from similar socio-economic backgrounds do in education and the labour market.

Young people brought up in Stirling, Falkirk and East Ayrshire had the most promising prospects in Scotland placing in the top 5-15% band of all local areas in the UK. This means they tended to have higher qualifications, earned more, and were in higher occupation levels by 25 to 44 years old compared with young people from similar socio-economic backgrounds across the UK.

Across the 32 local authorities in Scotland, results on the Index of Promising Prospects show most areas falling at or above the national average:

- Top 5% band: no areas
- Top 5-15% band: 3 area
- Top 15-30% band: 6 area
- 30-70% band: 16 areas
- Bottom 30-15% band: 1 area
- Bottom 15-5% band: 1 area
- Bottom 5% band: 5 areas

## Conditions of Childhood

This index shows whether the conditions children grow up in are good for promoting social mobility.

Children growing up in East Renfrewshire and East Dunbartonshire experienced the most favourable conditions of childhood in the Scotland region, placing in the top 5% of all local areas in the UK. This means parents in this area have higher levels of earnings, qualifications and professional roles. Conversely, children in the City of Edinburgh were in the bottom 15-5%  of all local areas in the UK, meaning they faced less favourable childhood conditions compared with the national average.

Across the 32 local authorities in Scotland, results on the Index of Conditions of Childhood show great variation, with 4 areas at the top 15%, 3 areas around the average, and 4 areas at the bottom 30%. 

- Top 5% band: 2 areas
- Top 5–15% band: 2 areas
- Top 15–30% band: 8 areas
- 30–70% band: 15 areas
- Bottom 30-15% band: 4 areas
- Bottom 15-5% band: 1 area
- Bottom 5% band: no areas

## Labour Market Outcomes

This index shows which areas offer better or worse employment opportunities for young people.

Young people brought up in areas like Moray, Dundee City and Shetland had the most favourable employment opportunities in Scotland, placing in the top 5-15% of all local areas in the UK. This means they have more professional opportunities and higher earnings. Conversely, young people in Glasgow City and the City of Edinburgh were in the bottom 30-15% of all local areas in the UK..

Across the 32 local authorities in Scotland, results on the Index of Labour Market Outcomes shows almost half of areas falling at or above the national average:

- Top 5% band: no areas
- Top 5-15% band: 6 areas
- Top 15–30% band: 17 areas
- 30–70% band: 7 areas
- Bottom 30-15% band: 2 area
- Bottom 15-5% band: no areas
- Bottom 5% band: no areas

## Innovation and Growth

This index shows which areas help promote innovation and economic growth.

The City of Edinburgh led in innovation and growth in Scotland placing in the top 5% of all local areas in the UK. This means that these areas benefited from higher productivity levels, and a higher proportion of strong &lsquo;new economy&rsquo; roles and postgraduate qualified residents. Conversely, Aberdeen City was in the bottom 15-5% of all local areas in the UK.

Across the 32 local authorities in Scotland, results on the Index of Innovation and Growth show most areas falling above or around the national average:

- Top 5% band: 1 area
- Top 5-15% band: 4 areas
- Top 15–30% band: 11 areas
- 30–70% band: 13 areas
- Bottom 30-15% band: 2 areas
- Bottom 15-5% band: 1 area
- Bottom 5% band: no areas

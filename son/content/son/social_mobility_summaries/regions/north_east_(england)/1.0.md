All areas are scored according to how favourable their environments are for the following 4 measures of social mobility: 

- Promising prospects
- Conditions of childhood
- Labour market opportunities for young people
- Innovation and growth.

Each measure of social mobility combines data from multiple indicators into a single &lsquo;composite&rsquo; score. The scoring bands range from &lsquo;Bottom 5%&rsquo; to &lsquo;Top 5%&rsquo;, with a large majority of areas in the &lsquo;30% to 70%&rsquo; band because most local authorities are very near the average.

## Promising prospects

This index measures how well young people from similar socio-economic backgrounds do in education and the labour market.

Young people brought up in Redcar and Cleveland and Newcastle upon Tyne had high promising prospects in the North East placing in the top 15 to 30%  of all local areas in the UK. This means they tended to have higher qualifications, earned more, and were in higher occupation levels by 25 to 44 years old compared with young people from similar socio-economic backgrounds across the UK.

Across the 12 local authorities in the North East of England, results show most areas falling above the national average:

- top 5% band: no areas
- top 5 to 15% band: 6 areas
- top 15 to 30% band: 2 areas
- 30 to 70% band: 1 areas
- bottom 30 to 15% band: no areas
- bottom 15 to 5% band: no areas
- bottom 5% band: 3 areas

## Conditions of childhood

This index shows whether the conditions children grow up in are good for promoting social mobility.

Children growing up in Sunderland,Hartlepool and Redcar and Cleveland experienced the most favourable conditions of childhood in the East of England region, placing in the top 5 to 15% of all local areas in the UK. This means parents in this area have higher levels of earnings, qualifications and professional roles. 

Across the 12 local authorities in the North East of England, results show great variation, with 3 areas at the top 15%, 8 areas around the average, and 1 area at the bottom 5%. 

- top 5% band: no areas
- Top 5–15% band: 3 areas
- Top 15–30% band: 5 areas
- 30–70% band: 3 areas
- bottom 30 to 15% band: no areas
- bottom 15 to 5% band: no areas
- bottom 5% band: 1 area

## Labour market outcomes

This index shows which areas offer better or worse employment opportunities for young people.

Young people brought up in areas like Durham,Middlesbrough and Stockton-on-Tees had the most favourable employment opportunities in the East of England region, placing in the top 5 to 15% of all local areas in the UK. This means they have more professional opportunities and higher earnings. Conversely, young people were in the 30 to 70% band of all local areas in the UK..

Across the 12 local authorities in the North East of England, results show over half of areas falling above the national average:

- top 5% band: no areas
- top 5 to 15% band: 3 areas
- Top 15–30% band: 8 areas
- 30–70% band: 1 areas
- bottom 30 to 15% band: no areas
- bottom 15 to 5% band: no areas
- bottom 5% band: no areas

## Innovation and growth

This index shows which areas help promote innovation and economic growth.

Hartlepool and Redcar and Cleveland led in innovation and growth in the North East region, placing in the top 5 to 15% of all local areas in the UK. This means that these areas benefited from higher productivity levels, and a higher proportion of strong &lsquo;new economy&rsquo; roles and postgraduate qualified residents. 

Across the 12 local authorities in the North East of England, results show most areas falling above the national average:

- top 5% band: no areas
- top 5 to 15% band: 2 areas
- Top 15–30% band: 7 areas
- 30–70% band: 3 areas
- bottom 30 to 15% band: no areas
- bottom 15 to 5% band: no area
- bottom 5% band: no areas

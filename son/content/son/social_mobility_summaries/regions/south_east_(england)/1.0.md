All areas are scored according to how favourable their environments are for the following 4 measures of social mobility: 

- Promising prospects
- Conditions of childhood
- Labour market opportunities for young people
- Innovation and growth.

Each measure of social mobility combines data from multiple indicators into a single &lsquo;composite&rsquo; score. The scoring bands range from &lsquo;Bottom 5%&rsquo; to &lsquo;Top 5%&rsquo;, with a large majority of areas in the &lsquo;30% to 70%&rsquo; band because most local authorities are very near the average.

## Promising prospects

This index measures how well young people from similar socio-economic backgrounds do in education and the labour market.

Young people brought up in Windsor and Maidenhead, Surrey, Slough, Milton Keynes, Oxfordshire and Bracknell Forest the most promising prospects in the East of England, placing in the top 5% of all local areas in the UK. This means they tended to have higher qualifications, earned more, and were in higher occupation levels by 25 to 44 years old compared with young people from similar socio-economic backgrounds across the UK.

Across the 19 local authorities in the South East of England, results show most areas falling at or above the national average:

- top 5% band: 6 area
- top 5 to 15% band: no areas
- top 15 to 30% band: 3 areas
- 30 to 70% band: 6 areas
- bottom 30 to 15% band: no areas
- bottom 15 to 5% band: 4 areas
- bottom 5% band: no areas

## Conditions of childhood

This index shows whether the conditions children grow up in are good for promoting social mobility.

Children growing up in Slough, Portsmouth, Medway, Kent and East Sussex experienced the most favourable conditions of childhood in the East of England region, placing in the top 5% of all local areas in the UK. This means parents in this area have higher levels of earnings, qualifications and professional roles. Conversely, children in Reading, West Berkshire and Brighton and Hove were in the bottom  15 to 5% of all local areas in the UK, meaning they faced less favourable childhood conditions compared with the national average.

Across the 19 local authorities in the South East of England, results show great variation, with 5 areas at the top 5%, 5 areas around the average, and 3 areas at the bottom  15 to 5%.

- top 5% band: 5 areas
- Top 5–15% band: no areas
- Top 15–30% band: 2  areas
- 30–70% band: 5 areas
- bottom 30 to 15% band: 4 areas
- bottom 15 to 5% band: 3 area
- bottom 5% band: no areas

## Labour market outcomes

This index shows which areas offer better or worse employment opportunities for young people.

Young people brought up in Bracknell Forest had the most favourable employment opportunities in the South East of England region, placing in the top 5% of all local areas in the UK. This means they have more professional opportunities and higher earnings. 

Across the 19 local authorities in the South East of England, results shows almost half of areas falling at or above the national average:

- top 5% band: 1 area
- top 5 to 15% band: no areas
- Top 15–30% band: 3 areas
- 30–70% band: 9 areas
- bottom 30 to 15% band: 6 areas
- bottom 15 to 5% band: no areas
- bottom 5% band: no areas

## Innovation and growth

This index shows which areas help promote innovation and economic growth.

Wokingham, Slough, Reading and Oxfordshire led in innovation and growth in the South East of England region, placing in the top 5% of all local areas in the UK. This means that these areas benefited from higher productivity levels, and a higher proportion of strong &lsquo;new economy&rsquo; roles and postgraduate qualified residents.

Across the 19 local authorities in the South East of England, results show most areas falling around or above the national average:

- top 5% band: 4 areas
- top 5 to 15% band: no areas
- Top 15–30% band: 3 areas
- 30–70% band: 5 areas
- bottom 30 to 15% band: 3 areas
- bottom 15 to 5% band: 4 area
- bottom 5% band: no areas

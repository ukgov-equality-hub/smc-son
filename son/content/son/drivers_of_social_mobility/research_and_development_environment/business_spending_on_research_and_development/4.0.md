[TOC]

## Summary main findings

From 2014 to 2024, spending on research and development by UK businesses went up from £41.0 billion to £55.6 billion, after adjusting for inflation. It was highest in 2021 at £55.8 billion.

Between 2023 and 2024 there has been an increase of £1.3 billion – the largest year-on-year increase since 2020 to 2021.

Out of 41 UK regions, business spending on research and development in 2024 was:

* highest in Inner London West at £789.7 million for every 100,000 residents
* lowest in Outer London - East and North East at £1.5 million for every 100,000 residents

## About the data

### Data sources
* Business Expenditure on R&D (BERD), Office for National Statistics
* Nomis population estimates, Office for National Statistics

### Time period
* 2014 to 2024 (By year)
* 2024 (By area)

### Geographic area
UK

### What the data measures
The data shows how much UK businesses spent on research and development between 2014 and 2024. It also shows spending for every 100,000 people in 41 regions of the UK.

### Things you need to know
Data over time has been adjusted for inflation using the R&D deflator, with 2024 as the base year. Following [changes to ONS’s methodology](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/nationalaccounts/satelliteaccounts/articles/deflatorimprovementstotheuknationalaccounts/bluebook2025) in November 2025, this series now uses the specific R&D deflator – previous versions of this page used the general GDP deflator. 

To maintain a consistent trend, data from 2014 to 2017 has been re-indexed to the 2024 baseline using a ‘link-year method’ (anchored in 2018). This preserves historical growth rates while ensuring the data aligns with the latest ONS R&D price indices and sampling improvements.

### Type of data
Administrative data

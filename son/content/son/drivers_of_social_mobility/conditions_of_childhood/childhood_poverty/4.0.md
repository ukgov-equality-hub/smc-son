## Summary main findings

In the 3 financial years to March 2025, the percentage of children living in relative poverty in the UK was stable at around 28%. 

Rates varied in different countries in the UK. In the 3 financial years to March 2025, the percentage of children living in relative poverty:

* went up in Wales, from 29% to 32%
* went down in Scotland, from 25% to 21%
* stayed roughly the same in England, at around 28%
* stayed roughly the same in Northern Ireland, at 18% to 19%

## About the data

### Data source
Department for Work and Pensions (DWP), Households Below Average Income statistics

### Time period
April 1996 to March 2025 (29 financial years)

### Geographic area
UK

### What the data measures
The data shows the percentage of children living in households in relative poverty after housing costs in the UK. It includes data for England, Northern Ireland, Scotland and Wales.

A household is in relative poverty if their equivalised income is below 60% of the median income for all households in the UK. ‘Equivalised’ means adjusted for the number and ages of the people living in the household.

### Things you need to know
The data covers financial years running from April to the following March. For example, the financial year 2024 to 2025 ran from April 2024 to March 2025.

Data is calculated using 3-year averages. For example, the figure for 2024 to 2025 is the average of the 3 financial years between April 2022 and March 2025. 

Due to a [change in methodology](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/households-below-average-income-for-financial-years-ending-1995-to-2025/households-below-average-income-background-information-and-methodology-report-fye-2025) from 2022 onwards, this data is not comparable with previous years’ data, or previous versions on this website. Data for 2021 to 2022 is not available due to disruptions during the COVID-19 pandemic. 

### Type of data
Survey data

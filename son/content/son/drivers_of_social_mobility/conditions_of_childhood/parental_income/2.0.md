Data on the real average (mean) hourly earnings of people with dependent children.

[TOC]

## Summary main findings

In 2025, the average (mean) income of people with dependent children was £23.89 an hour – up from £22.29 in 2024. This is the largest year-on-year increase since 2019 to 2020. 

Between 2018 and 2020, the figure went up from £20.63 to £23.69. Then it fell between 2020 and 2023, from £23.69 to £22.13. 

Mean hourly income for parents was:

* highest in west Inner London (£35.24 an hour)
* lowest in Cornwall and the Isles of Scilly (£17.60 an hour)

## About the data

### Data source
Labour Force Survey (LFS), Office for National Statistics

### Time period
* 2014 to 2025 (By year)
* 2014 to 2025 combined (By area)

### Geographic area
UK

### What the data measures
The data shows the average (mean) hourly earnings of people in the UK with dependent children.

This includes:

* opposite or same sex couples in marriages or civil partnerships
* opposite or same sex couples who live together
* single male or female parents

Dependent children are classed as:

* all children aged 15 and under
* 16 to 18 year olds in full-time education

Adult respondents aged under 21 years old are not included.

### Things you need to know
Data is weighted using LFS income weights.

Earnings have been adjusted for inflation using 2025 as the base year.

The error bars show 95% confidence intervals. Read more about [confidence intervals].

For this measure, we report the mean average for parental income. This is to maintain methodological consistency with the other indicators used to calculate the composite indices. Mean earnings data may be affected by a small number of very high earners. Because of this, it may be higher than the income of the 'typical' household using the median.

### Type of data
Survey data

Read more in [State of the Nation 2026 on GOV.UK].

Data on the percentage of 5 year olds who achieve a ‘good level of development’, by socio-economic background.

[TOC]

## Summary main findings

In the 2024 to 2025 school year:

* 68% of children achieved a good level of development
* 51% of pupils eligible for free school meals (FSM) and 73% of non-eligible pupils achieved a good level of development
* girls continued to outperform boys, with the gap between them remaining steady since the 2021 to 2022 school year
* the gap between girls and boys was 15 percentage points for FSM-eligible pupils and 13 percentage points for non-FSM eligible pupils
* in every ethnic group, FSM-eligible girls were much more likely to achieve a good level of development than FSM-eligible boys from the same ethnic background
* among FSM-eligible children, those from Black, Asian, and Mixed ethnic backgrounds were more likely to achieve a good level of development than White children (with pupils in the White and Other ethnic groups having the lowest rates)
* FSM-eligible pupils in parts of London, the West Midlands, and East Yorkshire and Northern Lincolnshire were the most likely out of all areas to achieve a good level of development – those in Cumbria, Herefordshire, Worcestershire and Warwickshire, North Yorkshire, and Bedfordshire and Hertfordshire were the least likely

There was a link between income deprivation and children achieving a good level of development. Neighbourhoods with higher rates of children living in income-deprived households had a lower percentage of children achieving a good level of development. In the 2024 to 2025 school year, the percentage of children achieving a good level of development was:

* 78% in the least deprived neighbourhoods
* 59% in the most deprived neighbourhoods

This was a 1 percentage point increase for both groups compared to the previous year.

In the 7 school years ending in July 2019, the percentage of 5-year-olds achieving a good level of development went up.

Due to the COVID-19 pandemic, data collection restarted in the 2021 to 2022 school year, and the data is not comparable to previous years.

## About the data

### Data source
Department for Education (DfE). Early years foundation stage (EYFS) profile results from the 2024 to 2025 academic year, 2025.

### Time period
* 13 school years to July 2025 (By year)
* 2024 to 2025 school year (By area, sex, ethnicity, and level of neighbourhood deprivation)

### Geographic area
England

### What the data measures
The data shows the percentage of pupils who achieved a ‘good level of development’ at 5 years old.

A child is classed as having a good level of development if they meet at least the expected standard in the early years foundation stage (EYFS).

### Things you need to know
Data is only available for England.

The EYFS was updated in September 2021. This means data for the 4 school years ending in July 2025 is not directly comparable with data for earlier years.

Data was not collected during the 2 school years ending in July 2021 due to the COVID-19 pandemic.

Eligibility for free school meals (FSM) is used as an indicator of deprivation by DfE. Children are eligible for FSM if their parents receive certain benefits.

Income deprivation affecting children index (IDACI) deciles are based on the percentage of children living in income-deprived households within a certain neighbourhood. These neighbourhoods are put into 10 groups (‘deciles’). Decile 1 is made up of the most deprived 10% of neighbourhoods – decile 10 is the least deprived 10% of neighbourhoods.

### Type of data
Administrative data

Read more in [State of the Nation 2026 on GOV.UK].

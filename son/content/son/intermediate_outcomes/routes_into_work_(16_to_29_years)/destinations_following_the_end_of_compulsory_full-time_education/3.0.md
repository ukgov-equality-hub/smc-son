Data on young people aged 16 to 24 years in the UK who were in education and training, employment or NEET (not in employment, education or training), by socio-economic background.

[TOC]

## Summary main findings

In 2025, young people aged 16 to 24 years from lower professional backgrounds were the most likely to be in education and training out of all socio-economic backgrounds. Young people from lower working class backgrounds were the most likely to be NEET (25%) and the least likely to be in employment (41%).

In the 3 years to 2025, the percentage of young people who were NEET was:

* 24% for those from lower working class backgrounds
* 9% for those from higher professional backgrounds

This gap has remained similar since the 3-year period to 2016.

The NEET rate for young people from higher working class backgrounds is increasing more steeply than for any other socio-economic background group, particularly since the 3-year period to 2022.

### Sex 
Social class differences in the percentages of young people in education and training, employment or NEET were similar among young men and women. When comparing the 2014 to 2019 combined period to 2020 to 2025, the percentage of young men who were NEET went up more than the percentage of women.

In 2014 to 2019 combined, young men and women participated in education and training at similar rates across all socio-economic backgrounds, but in 2020 to 2025, women overtook men in nearly every socio-economic group.  

For young people from lower working class backgrounds, 34% of men were in education and training compared with 31% of women. This was the only group where the percentage was higher for men than women in the 2020 to 2025 combined period.

### Ethnicity
Out of all ethnic groups, the percentage of young people from lower working class backgrounds who were NEET was highest in the Mixed (26%), Black Caribbean (25%), and White British (23%) ethnic groups. It was lowest in the Chinese (18%) and Indian (19%) ethnic groups. 

### Disability
Young people with a disability were much less likely to be in employment and much more likely to be NEET than those without a disability across all socio-economic backgrounds. Participation rates in education and training were similar between disabled and non-disabled young people. 

Comparing 2014 to 2019 with 2020 to 2025, the percentage of people from lower working-class backgrounds in education and training went up from 28% to 36% for disabled people. For non-disabled people, it went up from 31% to 32%.

## About the data

### Data source
Office for National Statistics, Labour Force Survey (LFS)

### Time period
* 2025 (By socio-economic background)
* 2014 to 2025, 3-year averages (Changes over time)
* 2014 to 2019 and 2020 to 2025 (By sex and disability status)
* 2014 to 2025 combined (By ethnicity)

### Geographic area
UK

### What the data measures
The data shows the percentage of young people aged 16 to 24 years in the UK who were in education and training, employment or ‘NEET’ (not in employment, education or training).

NEET is defined as not in employment, education or training in the week before the survey.

Socio-economic background (SEB) is based on the main earner’s occupation when the respondent was aged 14 years. Where there was no earner in the family, SEB is included in the lower working class.

### Things you need to know
The statistics are from the Labour Force Survey. They reflect the raw percentages and counts within our data. A percentage gap or directional change in these charts does not guarantee a statistically significant result. This is because variations within a sample can often reflect natural sampling changes rather than a systematic trend in the whole population.

The findings are not comparable to those from previous years. This is due to changes in our model and how we created the ethnicity and NEET variables.

The data used is weighted using the LFS person weights.

For changes over time, we use a 3-year moving average. The data covers the period from 2014 to 2025. The first data point covers the period from 2014 to 2016, and the final data point covers 2023 to 2025.

Data is combined to get more accurate estimates. Data by sex and disability is combined into 2 time periods to monitor change over time:

* 2014 to 2019
* 2020 to 2025

Due to a limited sample size, data by ethnicity is combined from 2014 to 2025.

For data by ethnicity, the estimated percentages shown are those for men. We show percentages only for those with lower working-class and higher professional backgrounds for illustrative purposes. Due to small sample sizes, we have not included analysis exploring change over time for data by ethnicity.

For data by disability status, respondents were asked if they had a health condition or illness lasting 12 months or more, and if that condition reduced their ability to carry out day-to-day activities. This is consistent with the definition of disability in the Equality Act 2010.

### Type of data
Survey data

Read more in [State of the Nation 2026 on GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/state-of-the-nation-2026-whos-getting-ahead).
